# Feature review

Review whether the feature helps the person make the decision or take the action it exists to support. A broad review can find a gap in the feature's purpose, behavior, or presentation even when the UI matches its design. Keep the review within the requested surface and follow adjacent steps when they change the judgment.

1. **Establish the feature's job.** Identify who uses it, what brings them there, what outcome they need, and what this item contributes. Ground the intended behavior in the brief, product rules, research, or an explicit owner decision. Use the current UI as evidence of what it presents or does. If purpose or policy is unsettled, state the working interpretation and the decision it leaves open. Check whether the item needs to exist in this place when its contribution is unclear.
2. **Derive the person's questions.** For consequential moments, ask what they need to know, distinguish, or do. Consider what they can do now, what an action will change, how they will recognize the outcome, and how they can recover or resume. Use the questions the actual task calls for.
3. **Trace the answers to the design.** Follow a realistic task through the available mock or live UI. For each important question, identify where and when the person gets an answer through information, a visible cue, an action, or feedback. Include states that change the answer and input methods that affect access. Use `references/modules/interaction-design/guide.md` to trace behavior and `references/modules/content-architecture/guide.md` or `references/modules/design-for-recognition/guide.md` to examine how the UI conveys it. A static mock establishes visible cues. Verify transitions in a runnable surface when available and label any unobserved behavior.
4. **Report the consequence and next decision.** Lead with the most consequential unanswered or misleading question. Give the state, observed evidence, effect on the task, and a proposed change or decision. Distinguish presentation gaps, behavior gaps, unsettled product rules, and any findings about fidelity to an approved design. Label expert judgment and participant evidence by source. Return findings at the requested scope. Edit production UI or authoritative documents only when that work is in scope.

For a session row, the task might be picking which session needs attention next. Check whether the person can identify the current session, distinguish unread activity from review readiness, and understand what a count conveys before opening the row. A mock can expose an unexplained count. What the count measures and what clears unread need a product source or a checkable behavior decision.
