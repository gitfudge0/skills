2
Two workers can grab the same retry job and both charge the customer.
what's happening
When a job's visibility timeout expires while it's still running, a second worker is allowed to pick up the same job and start its own charge attempt, because nothing marks the job as claimed until after the charge succeeds.
why it matters
Under load, a slow charge call can outlast the visibility timeout often enough that this isn't a rare edge case — the customer sees two charges for one order, and the reconciliation job has no way to tell which attempt was the "real" one.
the fix
Claim the job with a conditional update before making the charge call, the same pattern the settlement worker already uses for its ledger writes, so a second worker's claim fails fast instead of racing to charge.

3
Backoff delay is computed from the wrong timestamp after a process restart.
what's happening
The exponential backoff timer is calculated from an in-memory value that resets to zero whenever the worker process restarts, rather than from a value stored alongside the job.
why it matters
A deploy or crash mid-backoff makes every in-flight retry immediately eligible again, so a customer's failed payment can be retried in a tight loop right after a routine deploy instead of on the intended schedule.
the fix
Persist the next-eligible-at timestamp on the job record itself and read it back on restart, instead of keeping it only in the worker's local state.
